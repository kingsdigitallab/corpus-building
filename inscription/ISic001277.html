commentary

This inscription is highly fragmentary: most editors have interpreted it as a funerary inscription. Torremuzza’s reading (1784 cl. XVIII no. 33) of l. 1 ΘΗ, together with the presence of two genitives in ll. 3–4, leaves no room for alternative interpretations: θήκη is also used in other funerary inscriptions in the sense of “tomb” (e.g. IG XIV 633, 783). Ferrua (1989, 113–114 no. 428) identifies this term as a Christian word and integrates a christogram at the end of the inscription. Kaibel’s reading (ap. IG XIV 452) of l. 1 Θε[οῖς πᾶσι, besides being erroneous, is at the root of an incorrect interpretation of the document’s typology, which the scholar considered to be a dedicatory epigraph; this interpretation, however, conflicts with the genitives at ll. 3–4. It is therefore preferable to understand the text as an epitaph, for a deceased individual whose name is only partially preserved and which scholars have variously restored as Ἀρτέμων (Franz ap. CIG III 5572; Manni Piraino ap. IGMusPalermo 6) or Ἀρτεμίσιος (Sandberg 1954, 41), and for one of his relatives (the son according to Franz ap. CIG III 5572; the brother according to Sandberg 1954, 41; or the grandson according to Manni Piraino ap. IGMusPalermo 6). The last line has received several readings: Kaibel (ap. IG XIV 452), assuming it to be a dedication, read εὐπλοίας ἔνεκα, the expression used to thank the gods for a safe voyage, whereas Alföldi (1976, 511), although agreeing on the funerary destination of the text, was perhaps influenced by Kaibel’s interpretation and read a proper name (Εὔπλους or Εὐπλία), understood as the dedicant. For Ferrua (1989, 113–114 no. 428), this was the proper name of the deceased’s son, Εὔπλου. Sandberg (1954, 41–42), supported by Manni Piraino (ap. IGMusPalermo 6), read the imperative εὔπλοει or εὐπλοεῖτε as a wish addressed to the passer-by, similar to εὐτύχει or εὐτυχεῖτε: the plural form is also found in IG XIV 933 l. 11 (Portus, 1st–2nd century AD), while in the singular the contracted form εὐπλόι is more common (IG XIV 2409, 8; SEG 42.942 l. 2; IG XIV 2472 l. 9 = CIL XII 758).
