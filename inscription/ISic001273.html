commentary

This dedicatory inscription was made to Aphrodite, perhaps by three individuals, of whom the only partially preserved name is Archeboula (the name also appears in an inscription of the imperial period from Thermae Himeraeae: Kokalos 20 (1974), no. 17 (ISic001139)). According to Kaibel’s restoration (ap. IG XIV 448), the dedication would have been made by the Catanean boule and demos, however, in the absence of parallels for public dedications made by the Catanean civic institutions, Manganaro’s interpretation, according to which the dedication was made by private individuals, appears preferable. The cult of Aphrodite at Catania is attested by literary sources (the Pervigilium Veneris, which describes festivities that could be associated with the Catanean chora), numismatic evidence (some bronze coins from the Catanean territory dating between the 2nd and 1st centuries BC depicting the Goddess on the recto holding a dove), and epigraphic evidence, such as the dedication CIL X 2, 7013 (ISic000297), discovered near Motta S. Anastasia, in the Catanean chora, which mentions the cult of Venus Venetrix at Hybla Gereatis, a kome of Catania. The dating of the inscription is indicated in the first two lines by the mention of the priestess of Aphrodite, whose patronymic, Νικο]στράτου, was cautiously proposed by Manganaro (1994, 165 n. 115), a name rather widespread in imperial-period Sicily (cf. LGPN IIIA p. 327).
